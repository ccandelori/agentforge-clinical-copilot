# Hemoglobin A1C — Diagnostic and Interpretive Notes (Summary)

> **SOURCE:** American Diabetes Association *Standards of Care in
> Diabetes — 2024*, Section 2 ("Diagnosis and Classification of
> Diabetes"). Diabetes Care 47(Supplement_1).
>
> **NOTICE:** Short factual summary for the AgentForge demo corpus.
> Not clinical advice. Refer to the source for the full guidance.

## A1C as a Diagnostic Test

A1C is one of four equivalent diagnostic criteria for diabetes:

- **A1C ≥6.5%** (NGSP-certified, DCCT-aligned assay)
- Fasting plasma glucose ≥126 mg/dL (7.0 mmol/L) — fasting ≥8 hours
- 2-hour PG ≥200 mg/dL (11.1 mmol/L) during 75-g OGTT
- Random plasma glucose ≥200 mg/dL (11.1 mmol/L) with classic
  symptoms of hyperglycemia

Unless there is unequivocal hyperglycemia, the diagnosis requires
two abnormal test results — either two of the same test on separate
days, or two different tests at the same visit.

## Diagnostic Categories

| Category               | A1C            | FPG (mg/dL)        | 2-hr PG (mg/dL)    |
|------------------------|----------------|--------------------|--------------------|
| Normal                 | <5.7%          | <100               | <140               |
| Prediabetes            | 5.7–6.4%       | 100–125 (IFG)      | 140–199 (IGT)      |
| Diabetes               | ≥6.5%          | ≥126               | ≥200               |

Prediabetes is a risk state, not a milder disease — adults with
prediabetes should be counseled on lifestyle modification and
monitored annually.

## When NOT to Use A1C

A1C is unreliable in conditions affecting RBC turnover or hemoglobin
structure. Use plasma glucose instead in:

- Hemoglobinopathies (sickle cell disease/trait, HbC, HbE, HbS-β
  thalassemia)
- Recent significant blood loss or transfusion
- Hemolytic anemia
- Iron-deficiency anemia (under-treated)
- Erythropoietin therapy or iron repletion
- Pregnancy (second and third trimester) — use OGTT
- End-stage renal disease — A1C may underestimate
- Recent severe hyperglycemia (the A1C lags by ~3 months)

## A1C-to-Average-Glucose Conversion (eAG)

Approximate average glucose by A1C:

| A1C (%) | eAG (mg/dL) | eAG (mmol/L) |
|---------|-------------|--------------|
| 5.0     | 97          | 5.4          |
| 6.0     | 126         | 7.0          |
| 7.0     | 154         | 8.6          |
| 8.0     | 183         | 10.2         |
| 9.0     | 212         | 11.8         |
| 10.0    | 240         | 13.4         |
| 11.0    | 269         | 14.9         |
| 12.0    | 298         | 16.5         |

Formula: eAG (mg/dL) = 28.7 × A1C − 46.7.

## Interpretive Notes for the Agent

- **A1C trends matter as much as absolute values.** A patient moving
  from 6.4% to 7.5% over 6 months has worsening control even though
  both are "abnormal" — surface the trajectory, not just the latest
  result.
- **A1C must be interpreted alongside CBC.** Anemia (any cause) can
  shift A1C up or down; surface concurrent CBC results when
  available.
- **A new A1C ≥9% with prior values <7% is a meaningful clinical
  change** — escalate.
