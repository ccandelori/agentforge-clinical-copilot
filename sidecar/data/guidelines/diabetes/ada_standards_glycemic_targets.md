# ADA Standards of Care — Glycemic Targets (Summary)

> **SOURCE:** American Diabetes Association, *Standards of Care in
> Diabetes — 2024*, Section 6 ("Glycemic Goals and Hypoglycemia").
> Published in *Diabetes Care*, Volume 47, Supplement 1.
> https://diabetesjournals.org/care/issue/47/Supplement_1
>
> **NOTICE:** Short factual summary for the AgentForge demo corpus.
> Not clinical advice. Refer to the source for the full guidance.

## A1C Goal for Most Nonpregnant Adults

For many nonpregnant adults, an A1C goal of less than 7% is
reasonable. This target reduces the long-term risk of microvascular
complications (retinopathy, nephropathy, neuropathy) and, in
type 1 diabetes and early type 2 diabetes, macrovascular events.

The A1C goal should be individualized based on:

- **Duration of diabetes** — shorter durations support tighter targets
- **Life expectancy** — limited expectancy supports less stringent goals
- **Comorbid conditions** — extensive comorbidity supports less stringent goals
- **Established cardiovascular complications** — may support less stringent goals
- **Hypoglycemia unawareness** — less stringent goals reduce risk
- **Patient preferences and resources**

## Less Stringent A1C Goals

A1C goals less stringent than <7% (e.g., <8%) may be appropriate for
patients with:

- Limited life expectancy
- Advanced microvascular or macrovascular complications
- Extensive comorbid conditions
- Long-standing diabetes where the target has been difficult to
  achieve despite intensive efforts

## Glycemic Targets in Practice

For most nonpregnant adults with diabetes, the recommended targets are:

- **Preprandial (fasting) capillary plasma glucose:** 80–130 mg/dL
  (4.4–7.2 mmol/L)
- **Peak postprandial (1–2 hours after meal start) capillary plasma
  glucose:** less than 180 mg/dL (10.0 mmol/L)

## Hypoglycemia

Hypoglycemia is defined by glucose level alerts:

- **Level 1:** glucose 54–70 mg/dL (3.0–3.9 mmol/L) — alert value
- **Level 2:** glucose less than 54 mg/dL (less than 3.0 mmol/L) —
  clinically significant
- **Level 3:** severe event with cognitive impairment requiring
  external assistance

Avoiding level 2 and 3 hypoglycemia is essential for glycemic targets
to be considered safely achieved.
